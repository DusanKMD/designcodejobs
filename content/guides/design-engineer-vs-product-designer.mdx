# Design engineer vs product designer: skills, work and salary

A design engineer designs interfaces and ships them in production code. A product designer owns the problem, the flows, and the design, then works with engineers who build it. Both prototype and both care about craft. The difference is where the work ends: in a Figma file and a prototype, or in the codebase customers use.

This comparison uses 29 software design engineer postings from 19 companies and 147 product designer postings from 62 companies. They were pulled from the public Greenhouse, Ashby and Lever job board APIs of 163 tech companies on 10 November 2026. Eleven companies were hiring for both titles that day, so some comparisons below are like for like. Mechanical and hardware design engineering is a different job and is out of scope. Chip, antenna, and hardware product design engineer listings were removed from the sample.

## What is the difference between a design engineer and a product designer?

The difference between a design engineer and a product designer is ownership of the code. A product designer decides what to build and how it should behave, then partners with engineers to ship it. A design engineer makes many of the same design calls and also writes the production front end, usually in React and TypeScript.

Ashby’s co-founder and CTO puts the line plainly in the company’s [Design Engineer posting](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/Ashby/fd86edd7-3af0-4977-a61a-215212c296fa): “Design Engineer at Ashby isn’t just a Frontend Engineer with new branding, nor is it a Designer vibe coding prototypes.” The same company’s [Senior Product Designer posting](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/Ashby/f40ef345-82a8-4956-9150-193b4fdf8183) describes a different mix: “part designer, part product manager, part consultant, and part design system manager.”

So the product designer’s extra weight sits on the product side: problem framing, research, flows, and outcomes. The design engineer’s extra weight sits on the build side: components, front-end quality, and the design system in code. The overlap is real. The [what is a design engineer](/guides/what-is-a-design-engineer) guide covers how the title sits next to frontend engineering as well.

## What does each role do day to day?

A product designer spends the day framing problems, talking to users, mapping flows, designing screens, and testing prototypes with engineers and product managers. A design engineer spends more of the day in the codebase: building interfaces and components, prototyping in code, and polishing interaction details before they ship.

Ramp is hiring both titles in New York, and the two postings make the split concrete. The [Ramp Design Engineer](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/ramp/b68aca53-16c0-4ced-ab1d-e8beb2940b4f) “sit[s] at the intersection of design and front-end engineering,” will “prototype directly in code,” and builds “interactive prototypes in React and TypeScript.” Ramp wants people who “think like a designer, code like a front-end engineer.”

The [Ramp Product Designer](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/ramp/eca54d0e-232a-4c3e-bfcc-d6c6add393f5) is “accountable for outcomes, not just artifacts.” That role also prototypes with Cursor and Claude Code, but the posting says: “Let AI generate code while you guide structure, behavior, and UX quality. Partner with engineers to decide what moves into the product.” The designer prototypes. The engineers decide what ships in code.

Perplexity shows the same split on growth work. Its [Design Engineer, Growth & Marketing](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/Perplexity/fbbd496b-1d14-4396-b31d-38fbea0dc666) will “Own the work from Figma handoff to production” on a stack of React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS and Sanity. Its [Lead Product Designer, Growth](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/Perplexity/74a5eaeb-0a4b-412b-ac3a-ad73403b3fb7) will “Design and run experiments, using data and real usage to drive decisions and measure impact.” One owns the shipped page. The other owns the funnel and the decisions behind it.

## Which skills do design engineer and product designer postings ask for?

Design engineer postings ask for front-end code: React, TypeScript, and CSS appear in most of them and almost never in product designer postings. Product designer postings lean harder on research, usability testing, and storytelling. Both ask for prototyping, Figma, and design systems, though design engineers mention design systems more often.

The code rows are the clearest signal. Nine in ten design engineer postings name React. One in twenty product designer postings does. Design systems show up in both, but design engineers are usually asked to build them as components, not only to document them in Figma. Motion appears twice as often on the design engineer side, which matches the stress on interaction polish in the postings.

Product designer postings ask for a portfolio far more often. That does not mean design engineers skip one. Many design engineer postings ask for links to shipped work, GitHub, or live prototypes instead. The [design engineer portfolio guide](/guides/design-engineer-portfolio) covers what those should show.

## Do design engineers earn more than product designers?

Not reliably. On 10 November 2026, the median midpoint of posted US dollar base ranges was $237,500 for design engineers and $216,250 for product designers. But the design engineer sample is small, and at the same company the two titles often pay about the same. Level and company move pay more than the title does.

Only yearly base ranges that employers published were counted: 15 design engineer postings from 14 companies and 93 product designer postings from 43 companies. Equity, bonus, OTE, and hourly pay were left out, and nothing was converted between currencies. The median low end was $180,000 for design engineers and $184,000 for product designers. The median top end was $280,000 and $252,300. One design engineer posting, Y Combinator’s Software Product Design Engineer at $250,000 to $500,000, stretches the top. Without it, the design engineer median midpoint is $233,750.

## Which role is the right fit for you?

Choose design engineer if you want your work to end in merged code and you enjoy components, states, motion, and front-end detail. Choose product designer if you want to own the problem, the research, and the flow across a whole product area. Both paths need taste and close work with engineers.

Some questions help you decide:

- **Do you want to be judged on shipped UI or on product outcomes?** Design engineer postings stress production quality. Product designer postings stress outcomes, experiments, and metrics.
- **Would you rather review a pull request or a research plan?** Usability testing appears in 15% of the product designer postings and in none of the design engineer ones here.
- **How much do you like design systems?** Design systems appear in 72% of design engineer postings and 46% of product designer ones. Design engineers often own the coded component library. The [design systems engineer guide](/guides/design-systems-engineer) covers the role that goes furthest in that direction.
- **Do you code every week already?** If not, a product design role with code-based prototyping is a lower-risk way to test the fit.

Titles also blur. Gamma hires a UI Engineer, Pinterest and Reddit hire UX engineers, and Ideogram and OpusClip hire creative technologists into this same space. The [UX engineer guide](/guides/ux-engineer) and the [design technologist guide](/guides/design-technologist) explain those labels. Read the responsibilities, not the noun.

## How do you move from product designer to design engineer?

To move from product designer to design engineer, get your own components into a real codebase. Learn React, TypeScript, and CSS well enough that an engineer would merge your work. Then rebuild portfolio pieces as live code. Teams hiring design engineers want to see shipped UI, not only mockups or prototypes made in a design tool.

Some employers invite designers in directly. tldraw’s design engineer posting opens with “You’re a product designer who likes to build.” Ashby’s posting accepts experience “as an Engineer or a Designer.” Granola asks for either a frontend engineer with strong design skills or a designer with strong engineering skills. So the path exists, but the bar for code is real.

A practical sequence:

1. Pick one product you know and rebuild a small, finished part of it in React and TypeScript, including empty, loading, and error states.
2. Contribute to a design system in code, even an open-source one. Components are the most common shared ground between the two roles.
3. Pair with an engineer on your team and ship a small UI change through review.
4. Rewrite your resume around shipped interface work. The [design engineer resume checker](/tools/design-engineer-resume-checker) and the [portfolio checklist](/tools/design-engineer-portfolio-checklist) score both against what postings ask for.
