# What is a design engineer internship?

A design engineer internship, in software, is a student placement where you design product interfaces and build them in code: components, prototypes, and shipped UI. The title is almost never posted. On 24 November 2026, none of the 33 software design engineer postings on 396 public job boards was an internship, junior, or new-grad role.

This guide is about software and product design engineering: people who design interfaces and write the frontend code for them. It is not about mechanical, electrical, civil, or chip design engineering, which use the same phrase and fill most search results for it. The postings come from public Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby job boards, all checked on 24 November 2026.

## What is a design engineer internship?

A design engineer internship is a short paid placement, usually a summer, where a student works on a product team as both designer and frontend engineer. In practice the work comes under two other titles: frontend software engineering intern and product design intern. Both now ask for some of the other discipline, which is the design engineer’s core skill.

The full-time job it leads to is described in [what is a design engineer](/guides/what-is-a-design-engineer): one person who makes the visual and interaction decisions and writes the production code. Employers hiring that person rarely run a separate intern track for it. Ashby’s Design Engineer posting spells out what its entry level looks like. Its junior level is “no more than 2 years of industry experience as a designer or engineer,” with projects “with at least a couple of users” that show taste in both UI/UX and engineering. No junior posting was open at Ashby on 24 November 2026; its open Design Engineer and Staff Design Engineer postings are for experienced hires.

## Why do most design engineer intern listings ask for CAD?

Most listings titled design engineer intern are hardware jobs. Mechanical, structural, electrical, and chip teams also use the title design engineer, so a search for the phrase returns CAD, circuit, and building-systems internships. On the boards checked here, 132 of 165 design engineer titles were hardware, mechanical, chip, or data-center roles.

The two early-career postings with design engineer in the title on 24 November 2026 were both at Skydio, the drone maker: Product Design Engineer Intern and Product Design Engineer (Early Career). The intern posting asks for “Experience with 3D CAD. Familiarity with CATIA is highly valued.” and lists past projects such as gimbal vibration isolators. That is mechanical product design, not interface work.

The giveaway is the tool list. Skydio’s intern posting asks for 3D CAD and lists design for manufacturing among its bonus skills. Software design engineer roles ask for React, TypeScript, CSS, Figma, design systems, and a portfolio of shipped interfaces. This board lists only the second kind and filters hardware, chip, and audiovisual postings out.

## Are there software design engineer internships open now?

Not under that title. On 24 November 2026, 33 software design engineer postings were open at 22 employers on 396 public job boards, and none had intern, junior, or new grad in the title. The same boards carried 572 intern-titled postings at 80 employers, so internships exist; they sit under frontend engineering and product design.

The scan covered 32,647 open postings. Early-career postings in the two neighboring tracks:

- **Frontend or web engineering:** 9 intern or new-grad postings at 8 employers (DEPT, Epic Games, Lyft, Notion, Ramp, Skydio, Stripe, Verkada).
- **Product design:** 22 intern or new-grad postings at 11 employers (16 internships and 6 new-grad roles), led by Palantir with 10.

The closest single match was Figma’s PhD Intern, Software Engineer - Interaction Design (2027), in San Francisco or New York. The intern will “combine HCI research with hands-on-engineering to help define the next generation of interactive experience design tooling.” It is open only to fourth- or fifth-year PhD students.

## Which internships lead to design engineering?

Two internships lead most directly to design engineering: a frontend engineering internship where interface quality matters, and a product design internship that expects you to work in code. Pick by your home discipline. Developers should look for frontend roles that ask for an eye for design; designers should look for design roles that ask for HTML, CSS, or AI coding tools.

**Frontend internships that care about design.** Verkada asks for experience “building pixel perfect user interfaces” and an “Eye for design, product details.” Ramp wants “Strong JavaScript fundamentals and attention to visual and product details,” and its frontend stack includes Ryu, its in-house design system. Epic Games’ Web Engineer Intern builds features in React, Next.js, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS for its Tools & Portals team.

**Product design internships that touch code.** Notion’s Product Design Intern should have “a basic understanding of HTML and CSS” and be “comfortable working with coding agents (e.g. Cursor, Claude Code).” Coinbase’s intern will “Collaborate with engineers to build and ship PRs using AI-assisted coding tools, translating your designs into production-ready front-end changes.” Sigma’s intern will design with AI “through code directly or to generate prototypes.” Palantir says familiarity with HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and TypeScript “is appreciated.”

How the two full-time jobs differ from design engineering is covered in [design engineer vs frontend engineer](/guides/design-engineer-vs-frontend-engineer) and [design engineer vs product designer](/guides/design-engineer-vs-product-designer).

## How much do design engineer internships pay?

Posted rates for the nearest internships checked on 24 November 2026 run from $34 to $74 an hour in the US. Frontend internships post $47–$70 an hour at Skydio, $55–$65 at Verkada, and $57–$61 at Notion; Ramp posts $12,500 a month. Product design internships post $34–$70 an hour, and Palantir estimates $8,500 a month.

The low end is Databricks’ Product Design Intern, which posts $34–$42 an hour for Bellevue, Washington. Duolingo posts $43–$45 for its Product Designer, Thrive Intern in Pittsburgh, Figma $46 for its Product Design Intern, Patreon $47, Sigma $50, and Coinbase $60. Skydio pays its UX Product Design Intern the same as its frontend intern: $47 an hour for undergraduates, $53 for graduate students, and $70 for PhD students. The top of the set is Figma’s PhD interaction design internship at $74 an hour.

Some postings add support on top of pay. Figma’s internship postings offer “a housing stipend and travel reimbursement,” and Ramp adds a housing stipend to its monthly rate. These are a handful of US internships, not a market rate. For full-time pay after an internship, Databricks’ Product Design New Grad (2027 Start) posts a local range of $128,000–$140,000. The [design engineer salary guide](/guides/design-engineer-salary) covers how experienced roles are paid.

## What do you need to get a design engineer internship?

Most internships in this set want three things: current enrollment with a graduation date inside a set window, proof of work in a portfolio or GitHub, and working knowledge of the other discipline. Several also want a previous internship. Notion lists “Previous internship experience” for both its frontend and product design interns, and Verkada asks for internship experience launching products.

The enrollment rules are specific. Notion’s interns must graduate before summer 2028. Verkada wants candidates graduating by June 2028, and Ramp wants graduation in December 2027 or later. Palantir says its internship “should be your final internship before graduating.” Location rules apply too: Lyft’s Mexico City role requires a university in Mexico, and DEPT requires enrollment at a Dutch university for a five-month, full-time placement from February 2027.

What to show:

- **One interface you designed and built.** Palantir requires “a portfolio demonstrating at least one software interface design project.” Building it yourself, not only designing it, is what makes it design engineering.
- **Public code.** DEPT asks for “A GitHub with public repositories, or/and an online portfolio,” and wants semantic HTML, JavaScript, basic TypeScript, and React.
- **Accessibility basics.** DEPT asks that you know what “A11y” means. Covering keyboard and screen-reader use in a portfolio piece is a direct answer.
- **AI-assisted coding, used with judgment.** Notion and Coinbase both name AI coding tools in product design internships.

## How do you become a design engineer without that internship?

Most people reach design engineering through a frontend or product design internship, then a first job in that discipline, then a move once they can ship a feature alone. Ashby’s Design Engineer posting sets its mid level at “2+ years of continuous experience (e.g., internships don’t count),” so internships count as preparation, not experience.

The practical sequence is short. Take the internship your current skills fit. Learn the other half on real projects: React and CSS if you come from design, layout, typography, and interaction if you come from code. Ship something with real users, then apply to junior or design engineer roles when they open. Ashby’s junior definition asks for exactly that: projects with users that show taste in both design and code.
