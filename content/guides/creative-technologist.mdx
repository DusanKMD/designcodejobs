# What is a creative technologist?

A creative technologist is a designer-builder who uses code and new technology to make creative work: campaign assets, interactive experiences, brand tools, and the pipelines that produce them. In November 2026 that mostly means generative AI. Every one of the nine postings checked for this guide asks for AI work.

This guide covers what the role does in current postings, where creative technologists work, the skills employers list, how the title compares with design engineer and design technologist, posted pay, and how people get into it. The postings come from 238 public Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby job boards, all checked on 17 November 2026.

## What does a creative technologist do?

A creative technologist turns creative ideas into working technology. In current postings that means building AI workflows and tools that produce brand and campaign work, prototyping new formats before a team commits, and making some of the finished work by hand. Many also advise creative directors or clients on what a new tool can and cannot do.

The field the title comes from is wide. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_technology) describes creative technology as an interdisciplinary field “combining computing, design, art and the humanities,” and lists examples from computer graphics and video to virtual reality, augmented reality, and wearable technology. The 2026 job posts are narrower. Nine postings with the title were open on 17 November 2026, at eight employers, and all nine ask for generative AI or AI agents in the work itself.

Three kinds of work show up again and again:

- **Building production systems.** R/GA’s Creative Technology Director posting says the person will “build agent pipelines, MCP servers, plugins, generative video systems, and voice agents” for brands, and expects “at least 75% of your time to be spent in a repo.” The person in Lovable’s posting builds “the front-end surfaces and the AI-generation systems” that let its brand team produce at scale.
- **Making the work.** DEPT’s AI Motion Designer & Creative Technologist in Budapest turns briefs into AI-generated imagery, video, and motion, then finishes it in Photoshop, After Effects, and Premiere Pro. OpusClip’s lead role lists video editing craft as a “hard requirement.”
- **Judging tools.** Monks’ Creative Technologist in Buenos Aires evaluates and benchmarks generative AI models against each other to pick the right one for a brief, and checks briefs early for technical feasibility.

## Where do creative technologists work?

Creative technologists work in two places right now: agencies and creative-services firms, and AI product companies. Six of the nine postings checked on 17 November 2026 come from agencies or studios such as R/GA, Monks, Code and Theory, DEPT, and Superside. The other three come from AI companies: Ideogram, OpusClip, and Lovable, where the role sits close to marketing, brand, or the product itself.

The agency postings serve clients. Superside’s AI Creative Technologist turns “the requests our customers keep coming back with into automations and agents that run,” and trains customer teams to run them. Code and Theory’s Forward Deployed Creative Technologist in Bengaluru works with clients and AI partners to prototype and deploy AI-powered creative tools, including custom Photoshop and Figma plugins.

The AI-company postings face the company’s own product and audience. Ideogram’s hire operates as “a small, AI-powered creative studio inside our marketing team,” builds landing pages, and helps run social channels. OpusClip’s lead owns the video editing presets its AI Producer ships with, plus the public preset library creators install.

## What skills do creative technologist postings ask for?

These postings ask for a mix of visual taste, hands-on AI building, and enough code to ship. All nine postings checked on 17 November 2026 ask for AI work, and seven mention a portfolio. Beyond that the tool lists split by the kind of work: design and motion tools for making, scripting and agent tooling for building.

Tools named in the nine postings:

- **Adobe tools** (Creative Cloud, Photoshop, After Effects, or Adobe APIs): 5 postings.
- **Figma**: 4 postings, from design work to custom plugins.
- **ComfyUI** and other node-based AI workflows: 3 postings (Monks, Code and Theory’s US director role, DEPT).
- **AI coding tools** such as Cursor or Claude Code: 3 postings (Ideogram, Monks, Code and Theory).
- **Python**: 3 postings: R/GA’s stack, a plus at Code and Theory, and one scripting option at DEPT.
- **3D tools**: Blender or Cinema 4D in 2 postings, Three.js in 2.
- **React**: 2 postings, as a nice-to-have at Ideogram and in Lovable’s stack.

The code bar varies more than the title suggests. Monks expects working knowledge of JavaScript, TypeScript, CSS, and Tailwind, but says “deep traditional full-stack depth is not a hard requirement.” Lovable asks for “a track record of building, shipping, and maintaining real full-stack software.” DEPT asks for “some ability to build as well as make,” such as scripts, Figma plugins, or ComfyUI workflows that speed up creative work.

Taste is the other constant. Ideogram lists “exceptional visual taste” first. Monks asks for “high visual design sensitivity” combined with clean coding. R/GA wants work that “never looks like hastily generated slop.” The portfolio is how a team checks that.

## Is a creative technologist the same as a design engineer?

Sometimes, and some employers say so in the posting. Lovable’s listing for the title opens by asking for a design engineer and adds, “You might call yourself a design engineer, creative technologist or even a full-stack product engineer who cares about craft.” Where the titles split, creative technologist leans toward brand, campaign, and AI content work, and design engineer toward product interface.

Stripe shows the overlap from the other side. Its Creative Engineer role in the Stripe Creative Lab, open on 17 November 2026, describes the person as a design engineer who prototypes “experimental digital experiences, interactive projects, and creative tools.” It asks for 6+ years of “software engineering, creative technology, or creative development experience,” and prefers WebGL, WebGPU, Three.js, shaders, and physical computing such as Raspberry Pi or Arduino. The title is different. The skills sit squarely between the two.

On this board, design engineer is by far the more common title. On 17 November 2026 the board listed 38 open software design roles at 24 companies: 27 design engineer, 5 design systems engineer, 3 creative technologist, 2 UX engineer, and 1 UI engineer. The three listings with the title are [Ideogram’s](/jobs/ideogram-creative-technologist-e05672a2), [OpusClip’s](/jobs/opusclip-creative-technologist-69bb7a1e), and [Lovable’s](/jobs/lovable-creative-technologist-a4e39bdc). The [what is a design engineer](/guides/what-is-a-design-engineer) guide covers the product side in full.

The design technologist title is the closest neighbor. It usually sits in a design org and centers on prototypes and design-system code. The [design technologist guide](/guides/design-technologist) compares it with UX engineer, creative technologist, and prototyper.

## How much does a creative technologist make?

Only two of the nine postings checked on 17 November 2026 publish a base salary range. OpusClip’s lead role in Mountain View posts $195,000–$280,000. Code and Theory’s Director, Creative Technology in New York posts $160,000–$225,000, a range it says spans multiple levels. Both are senior roles, so neither is a typical figure for the title.

The other seven postings give no range, including every posting outside the US. For a nearby comparison, DEPT’s Creative Developer role in Toronto, a six-month fixed-term contract building motion-heavy front ends, posts CA$70,000–CA$80,000. That is a different title, but it shows how wide the spread under creative titles can be.

## How do you become a creative technologist?

Most people get into the role by building a portfolio that shows both creative output and working technology. Seven of the nine postings mention a portfolio, and several want proof that something shipped. Years of experience vary: three postings ask for 5 to 8 years, while DEPT and Monks put the work ahead of tenure.

The experience asks in the postings checked on 17 November 2026:

- **Superside:** 5+ years in agencies or in-house creative teams, “preferably with a background in design systems or creative technology.”
- **R/GA:** 6+ years in design, strategy, product management, or a related tech-forward environment.
- **Code and Theory, Bengaluru:** 7–8 years in creative technology, full-stack engineering, or technical product development.
- **DEPT:** “open to all levels of experience: what matters is the quality of your work and your judgement about when and how to use these tools.”
- **Monks:** optimizing for a track record with emerging technology “rather than sheer length of tenure.”

The portfolio asks are specific. R/GA wants “repos, deployed demos, working agentic systems.” OpusClip wants short-form edits first, with notes on “which pieces performed and why,” plus a social or GitHub profile where you build in public. Monks asks for examples that show “technical problem-solving, not just final polish.” A portfolio that shows one finished campaign and the tool or pipeline behind it answers both halves.

People reach the role from both sides. Designers and motion designers add scripting, plugins, and AI workflows. Front-end developers add motion, 3D, and visual craft.
