# What is a design engineer?

A design engineer, in software, builds the interface. They sit with product design and with frontend engineering, and the work is the overlap: a prototype that runs, a component that other people can reuse, and a shipped UI that still looks like the design. “What do design engineers do” is the same question. The day is spent turning interaction decisions into code, and code constraints back into design decisions.

The title also belongs to mechanical, industrial, civil, and chip design. Those engineers design physical parts, boards, and structures. Search results mix the two, because the words are identical. This guide is about the software job: designers who code, and the titles teams use when they hire them.

## What do design engineers do?

A design engineer turns an interface decision into code that ships. The day is a prototype, a reusable component, and the production UI: layout, states, motion, empty cases, and keyboard access. On a product team the same person often owns tokens and the design system other designers and engineers build on.

The useful description is concrete. A design engineer takes a flow that exists as a Figma file, a rough sketch, or a sentence in a ticket, and makes it work in the product. Often that means a prototype the same day, then the production version: layout, states, motion, empty and error cases, keyboard access, and the CSS that holds up when the content is longer than the mock.

They also build the shared surface. Design systems work is a large part of the job on product teams: tokens, component APIs, documentation, and the small decisions (focus rings, icon sizes, density) that make a product feel like one product. Brand and marketing teams use the same title for campaign sites and product pages, sometimes in Framer, sometimes in a custom React codebase. [Framer jobs](/skills/framer) on this board mean Framer the design tool.

The collaboration is specific. With a product designer, the question is whether the interaction is right. With a frontend or product engineer, the question is whether it can live in the existing app: data loading, performance, and the component boundaries already in the repo. The design engineer is often the person who can hold both conversations without translating through a third.

That mix is the job description in practice. A posting that asks only for CAD, GD&T, or a cleanroom is the other profession. A posting that asks for React, a design tool, and a system is this one.

## How does the role show up on a team?

Teams hire the same skills under three scopes: product UI, brand and web, and the design system. The scopes are not a ladder. A staff design engineer on a product can spend a quarter on the system, and a design systems engineer still ships interface code.

**Product.** The surface of the app: in-product UI, quality of interaction, and the design system other designers and engineers ship on. Most listings under [software design engineer jobs](/roles/design-engineer) are this shape.

**Brand and web.** Marketing sites, launch pages, and campaign work. The craft is still interface code, with more emphasis on motion, typography, and a page that has to be finished on a date. Linear’s open design engineer roles are filed as web and brand. Baseten and Perplexity have hired the title onto brand and growth surfaces.

**Systems.** Tokens, documentation, and components that have to work across products or platforms. See [design systems engineer jobs](/roles/design-systems-engineer). The title on the posting is often “design systems” plus engineer or frontend, not “design engineer” alone. Our guide explains [what a design systems engineer does](/guides/design-systems-engineer).

## Which titles do teams actually post?

Teams post this work as design engineer, UX engineer, UI engineer, design technologist, or creative technologist. The head term is the noisiest, because mechanical search uses the same words. The cleaner titles are the ones a software-only board can file without a disclaimer.

- [UX engineer jobs](/roles/ux-engineer). Closest to the role large companies have used for a designer-engineer who prototypes at high fidelity and builds the system. Read [what a UX engineer does](/guides/ux-engineer).
- [UI engineer jobs](/roles/ui-engineer), including postings that say UI developer. The emphasis is implementation quality: layout, states, and the component API.
- [Design technologist jobs](/roles/design-technologist). Often the bridge role: prototypes that are real enough for engineering to adopt, inside a design org. See [what a design technologist does](/guides/design-technologist).
- [Creative technologist jobs](/roles/creative-technologist). More often agency, marketing, or WebGL-heavy product work.

A software design engineer, a UX engineer, and a design technologist can be the same seat with a different org chart. Read the responsibilities, not the noun.

## How does the work sit next to nearby roles?

A design engineer owns the visual and interaction call and is expected to have taste about it. A frontend engineer owns more of the platform. A product designer owns the problem and the flows. The overlap is intentional, and the posting should say which seat it is.

A frontend engineer owns more of the platform: routing, data, performance, and features that are not primarily a design problem. A design engineer owns more of the visual and interaction call, and is expected to have taste about it, not only to implement a spec.

A product designer owns the problem, the flows, and the design. Many product designers prototype. The design engineer is the person for whom the prototype and the production UI are the main output, in the codebase the company ships.

The overlap is intentional. Hiring managers who want a pure frontend engineer, or a pure product designer, should say that in the title. Hiring managers who want both should say design engineer, UX engineer, or design technologist, and then show the stack. A template for that posting is the [design engineer job description](/guides/design-engineer-job-description).

## What does this board treat as the role?

This board keeps a listing when the title and the description match software interface work, and drops it when they match hardware, mechanical, chip, or AV design. Pay is shown only when the employer published a yearly base range. Remote means fully remote, not a hybrid week.

Pay, when the employer published a yearly base range, is on [design engineer salary](/salary). Remote roles are on [remote design engineer jobs](/remote). The salary guide explains how those ranges are counted, and what a single posting can and cannot say about the market.
