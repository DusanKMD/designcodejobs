# What is a UX engineer?

A UX engineer is a front-end developer who works inside a UX team. The job is to turn design and research questions into working code: prototypes a study can test, components a design system can share, and production UI that ships. Google runs a whole UX Engineering job family under the name, and teams also post the same seat as design engineer, design technologist, or UI engineer.

This guide covers what the work looks like in current postings, how the role differs from UX designer, front-end developer, and design engineer, the skills employers list, where UX engineers report, and what the board shows for open roles and posted pay.

## What does a UX engineer do?

A UX engineer writes the code that design and research depend on. That means coded prototypes for design reviews and user studies, shared components for the design system, and production UI features. The mix changes by team. Some UX engineers mostly prototype, and others spend most of the week in the product codebase.

Google’s [UX Engineering page](https://uxe.withgoogle.com/) lists a typical day in four lines: write and review production code for UI features, code prototypes for design projects, work with UX designers on different designs, and work with UX researchers on what features a study prototype needs. Research is the part that sets the title apart. A UX engineer builds the thing a participant clicks through, so the prototype has to behave like the product, not like a slide.

Current postings show the same split. Google’s [Senior UX Engineer, Chrome Desktop](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/126313276148458182-senior-ux-engineer-chrome-desktop) role, checked on 22 October 2026, asks the person to design and prototype new features and to “write and review code in Chrome’s codebase.” Reddit’s [Principal UX Engineer, Ads](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/reddit/jobs/8122887) posting describes someone who “sees code as a product-design medium” and builds functional prototypes, workflow tools, agents, and “PR-ready product experiences.”

AI work shows up in two of these three postings. Reddit’s asks for AI-native workflows, skills, and agents. Google’s [UX Engineer, Search Design System](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/109652666363060934-ux-engineer-search-design-system) role asks for prompt pipelines that make model output map to UI components, and lists AI Studio, Cursor, Antigravity, and Firebase Studio as preferred tools.

## Is a UX engineer the same as a design engineer?

Mostly, yes. Both titles describe a person who designs and codes interfaces, and employers count either as relevant experience. Mixpanel’s Staff Design Engineer posting asks for 8+ years “in design engineering, UX engineering, design technology, or a related hybrid role.” Where they differ, UX engineer leans toward research prototypes, and design engineer toward shipped product UI.

That line is from Mixpanel’s [Staff Design Engineer posting](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/mixpanel/jobs/8053868), which sits in the design organization. Google counts the titles as one pool too. Its Chrome Desktop posting prefers 6 years “as a front-end developer, UX Engineer, creative or design technologist, or in a prototyping design environment.” On this board, design engineer is by far the more common title: 29 of 35 open listings on 22 October 2026, against 1 UX engineer. The [what is a design engineer](/guides/what-is-a-design-engineer) guide describes that role in full.

Some people use the two titles for themselves. Donnie D’Amato, writing on [his blog](https://blog.damato.design/posts/terminal-career/) in December 2022, says peers called him a design engineer as a teenager, and he later became the first UX engineer at a real estate startup. His first tasks were prototypes for research studies. He then took over the platform’s CSS and the component library, which grew into a design system.

The design technologist title sits close to both. Our [design technologist guide](/guides/design-technologist) compares that title with UX engineer, creative technologist, and prototyper.

## How is a UX engineer different from a UX designer or a front-end developer?

A UX designer decides what the experience should be, mostly in design tools and research. A front-end developer builds the product’s client code, usually from finished designs and with more platform work. A UX engineer works between them: building the experience in code while the design is still open, so the team can test it.

The three roles share a lot of vocabulary, which is why search results mix them. The difference is in what each one is accountable for. D’Amato puts it this way: as a software engineer, he is expected to be responsible for databasing, CI/CD, and bundling configurations, while “as a UX Engineer, you are responsible for the user experience.”

Michelle Barker, writing on [CSS \{ In Real Life \}](https://css-irl.info/thoughts-on-ux-engineering/) in November 2023, describes the same line from the developer side. She calls her preferred work the “front-of-the-front end” and says she identifies with the title “UI and UX Engineer.” When a posting reads mostly like implementation quality, responsive layout, states, and component APIs, the title is often UI engineer. Those roles are on [UI engineer jobs](/roles/ui-engineer).

## What skills do UX engineer postings ask for?

UX engineer postings ask for years of front-end development or prototyping, fluent HTML, CSS, and JavaScript or TypeScript, and often React. They also ask for design judgment and work with designers and researchers. Senior postings add design-system ownership, accessibility, and AI-assisted prototyping. A portfolio that shows working code carries the application.

Three postings, all checked on 22 October 2026, show the range:

- **Google, Senior UX Engineer, Chrome Desktop** (Los Angeles). Asks for 6 years of experience in front-end development, technical UX design, or prototyping, and the “ability to translate mocks and wireframes into well-structured and responsive user interfaces.”
- **Google, UX Engineer, Search Design System** (Mountain View, New York, and other locations). Asks for 6 years in front-end development or UX engineering, plus HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. The work includes prototypes, design-system standards, and mentoring engineers and designers.
- **Reddit, Principal UX Engineer, Ads** (remote, United States). Asks for React and TypeScript, “production-adjacent prototypes,” reusable tools and components, and fluency with AI-assisted coding. It wants a portfolio that shows “technical architecture, systems thinking,” and work with engineering teams, “not only polished UI outcomes.”

The same skills appear under other titles on this board. On 22 October 2026, 24 of its 35 open listings mentioned prototyping in the description, 26 mentioned design systems, and 15 mentioned accessibility. None used the phrases user research or usability, which is the clearest gap between a typical design engineer posting and a UX engineer one. [Design system jobs](/skills/design-systems) and [React design engineer jobs](/skills/react) collect those listings. ## Where do UX engineers sit on a team?

Usually inside the UX or design org, working next to designers and researchers, with close ties to an engineering team that owns the production code. Some companies place the role in engineering instead. The reporting line matters, because it decides who reviews the work and whether a career ladder exists for it.

Google’s postings describe a “multi-disciplinary UX team” that collaborates with engineering and product management. Reddit’s role sits on the Ads Design and Research team and is asked to “help establish UX Engineering as a capability” there. In both cases the UX engineer is a specialist inside design, not a developer on loan from engineering.

D’Amato’s post is about what happens when that placement is not planned. He reported to a director of UX who had never managed an engineer, and there was no ladder for the role. He moved back to the engineering org, later changed companies, and at the time of writing his title was Software Engineer. His summary: “Often a company has a UX Engineering role but reports under either design or engineering.” Barker writes that she has been “under-valued and underpaid” compared with more technical colleagues in past roles.

For a candidate, the useful question in an interview is who writes the performance review, and against which ladder. For a hiring team, the [UX engineer job description section](/guides/design-engineer-job-description#what-belongs-in-a-ux-engineer-job-description) of our job description guide covers what to say in the posting.

## How many UX engineer jobs are open, and what do they pay?

Few, on this board. On 22 October 2026, one of its 35 open software listings had UX engineer in the title: Reddit’s Principal UX Engineer, Ads, remote in the United States. Its posted base range is $245,800 to $344,100 USD a year. One posting is a single employer’s band for a principal-level role, not a market rate.

The board’s counts on that date, from 35 open listings at 23 companies: 29 filed as design engineer, 3 as design systems engineer, 1 as creative technologist, 1 as UI engineer, and 1 as UX engineer. Two descriptions used the phrase UX engineer at all: Reddit’s, and Mixpanel’s Staff Design Engineer posting, which lists UX engineering as qualifying experience.

The title is more common at large companies. Google had at least two UX engineer postings open on 22 October 2026, the Chrome Desktop and Search Design System roles above. The board does not carry them yet, because it reads public Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby job boards and Google posts on its own careers site.

Salary search results are noisy for this title. On 22 October 2026, four of the top five organic US Google results for “ux engineer salary” were about UX designer or user experience design pay, which is a different job. This board shows pay only when an employer writes a yearly base range on the posting.
